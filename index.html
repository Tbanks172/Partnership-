The Creative Concept

So, what would we actually create together?

Not resort content with a relationship caption. Relationship stories that could only happen because two people finally got away. Not a room tour. Not a Reel pointing at the pool. Not "come spend 3 days with me at Secrets."

The property becomes the setting. The relationship remains the story.

Concept 01

Away From Everything. Back To Each Other.

A relationship storytelling series exploring what couples discover when the noise of normal life disappears — work, children, notifications, responsibilities, routines. And suddenly, it's just the two of them again.

"If I took away your phones, your jobs, your children, your routines and everything you normally talk about…"
"…would the two of you still know how to enjoy each other?"
Secrets Resorts & Spas × Tomiwa Bankole

The story unfolds through Tomiwa's existing viral conversation format. Only gradually does the audience discover the conversation is taking place during an intentional escape at Secrets. Secrets isn't inserted into the story — Secrets creates the environment that makes the story possible.

Concept 02

When Was The Last Time?

A visually intimate series built around questions couples rarely stop long enough to ask: breakfast without rushing, a day without needing to be anywhere else, dressing up just for each other.

"When was the last time you had breakfast together without rushing?"
"When was the last time you talked without checking a notification?"
"When was the last time you got away… just to be us again?"

The luxury isn't only where they are. It's having uninterrupted access to each other again.

Concept 03

The Question We Never Have Time To Ask

Each piece begins with one uncomfortable or unexpectedly intimate question. Not promotional. Not about Secrets. About them.

"Do you think we're still discovering each other… or have we started assuming we already know everything?"
"What part of you have I stopped being curious about?"
"If we met each other for the first time today, what would you want me to notice about you?"

The conversation unfolds against the property. Then, only at the end: maybe some relationships don't need another thing to do. Maybe they need enough quiet to notice each other again.
